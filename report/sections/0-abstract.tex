\begin{abstract}
Conventional multimodal affect understanding assumes that image and text provide complementary or semantically aligned signals. Memes fundamentally violate this assumption: their emotional meaning often arises from \emph{incongruity} between modalities, such as sarcastic text paired with a cheerful image. We argue that image-text incongruity is not noise, but a core signal for understanding meme emotion and pragmatic intent.

We present \textbf{MemeSonic}, an \textbf{incongruity-aware affective understanding framework} for memes. First, we explicitly model cross-modal incongruity by projecting image and text into independent emotion distributions and computing a continuous \emph{incongruity score} $\delta$. Based on this signal, we formulate meme understanding as an incongruity-aware reasoning problem and introduce \textbf{FusionMoE}, a mixture-of-experts architecture that dynamically adapts fusion strategies for redundancy, complementarity, and irony-dominant cases. Across ten tasks on three meme benchmarks (MET-Meme, Memotion~7k, MMSD~2.0), FusionMoE outperforms conventional fusion baselines by up to \textbf{+14.5\% Macro-F1}, with the largest gains on sarcasm and metaphor understanding.

Finally, we investigate whether expressive speech can serve as a \textbf{pseudo affective modality} for meme understanding. Since memes rarely contain annotated emotional voiceovers, we introduce synthesized expressive speech as a form of privileged affective supervision, providing cues about \emph{how the meme should be spoken}. A controlled tri-modal alignment study using Emotion2Vec embeddings shows that naive heterogeneous fusion yields negligible audio contribution, while a learned modality projector improves sentiment accuracy from \textbf{27\% to 82\%}. These results suggest that expressive speech captures affective information beyond image and text alone, demonstrating the value of externalizing multimodal incongruity into an explicit auditory signal for affective meme understanding.
\end{abstract}
