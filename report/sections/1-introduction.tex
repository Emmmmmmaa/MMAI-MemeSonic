\section{Introduction}

Conventional multimodal models are built on an implicit assumption: image and text convey the same
semantic signal, and combining them should yield a stronger representation. This assumption holds for
photo captions and visual question answering, but systematically breaks on memes.
Meme affect is constructed through \emph{incongruity}—a serene image paired with a desperate
caption, a cheerful face accompanying nihilistic text. The emotional charge does not reside in either
modality alone; it emerges from the \emph{tension} between them. Standard fusion approaches that pool
or align representations across modalities suppress exactly this signal.

We address this by making cross-modal incongruity an explicit, first-class variable.
We map image and text into independent emotion distributions and compute a continuous
\emph{incongruity score} $\delta$ that quantifies their divergence in affective space.
This score is not a byproduct—it is the primary routing signal for \textbf{FusionMoE}, a
Mixture-of-Experts classifier that selects the fusion strategy most suited to each sample's level of
cross-modal conflict. Samples with high $\delta$ (sarcasm, metaphor) are routed to a synergy expert
that exploits the conflict; samples with low $\delta$ use redundancy or uniqueness experts.
Across ten tasks on three meme benchmarks (MET-Meme, Memotion~7k, MMSD~2.0), FusionMoE outperforms
standard fusion baselines by up to \textbf{+14.5\%} Macro-F1, with the largest gains on tasks where
the conflict signal is semantically load-bearing.

Beyond classification, we externalize the implicit pragmatic affect in $\delta$ as expressive
voiceover by mapping the incongruity score to TTS prosody parameters. The generated speech
captures ironic or sarcastic delivery that text semantics alone cannot encode, and is introduced
as a \emph{pseudo affective modality} that provides additional supervisory signal for meme
understanding. A tri-modal alignment study confirms that speech carries non-redundant affective
information: a linear projector lifts sentiment accuracy from 0.27 to 0.82, and human evaluation
(n=84) shows 84.5\% preference over Vanilla TTS.

\textbf{Contributions.} (1) We introduce the incongruity score $\delta$ as an explicit architectural
primitive for meme affect modeling. (2) We propose FusionMoE, an incongruity-routed multi-expert
fusion classifier that adapts to cross-modal conflict at the sample level. (3) We generate expressive
meme voiceover by conditioning TTS prosody on $\delta$, and use the resulting audio as a pseudo
affective modality for tri-modal meme understanding.
