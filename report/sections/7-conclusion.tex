


\section{Conclusion}

We presented \textbf{MemeSonic}, a framework for meme affective understanding and expressive voiceover generation. The central insight is that image-text incongruity is not noise, but a structural cue for understanding meme affect and pragmatic intent. MemeSonic models this cue explicitly and further externalizes implicit pragmatic affect as expressive speech.

\emph{(1) Image-text incongruity improves meme affect understanding.}
Compared with standard multimodal fusion, MemeSonic better handles cases where image and text convey conflicting signals, especially in sarcasm, humor, and non-literal affect. This shows that meme meaning often emerges not from either modality alone, but from the tension between them.

\emph{(2) Expressive voiceover generation externalizes pragmatic affect.}
MemeSonic achieves the best EMOS and AMOS scores while maintaining comparable speech naturalness. This suggests that the learned prosody prompt adapter, conditioned on affect labels and image-text context, produces more meme-aligned delivery than direct label-guided or vanilla TTS.

\emph{(3) Generated speech provides non-redundant affective supervision.}
By using generated voiceover as a pseudo affective modality, MemeSonic introduces an additional supervisory signal for meme understanding. Our diagnostic tri-modal study suggests that affective information exists in speech.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
MemeSonic still relies on general-purpose vision and audio encoders, which may miss meme-specific visual semantics and expressive speech cues. In addition, humor, sarcasm, and pragmatic affect are inherently subjective, and existing datasets rarely provide aligned image, text, affect, intention, and audio annotations for the same memes. Our experiments should therefore be read as a focused validation of the proposed direction rather than a complete solution.

\paragraph{Future Work.}
A key next step is to build meme-native multimodal datasets with fine-grained annotations for affect, intention, humor, and paired voiceover audio. Such resources would support end-to-end training and evaluation of the full pipeline. Another direction is joint tri-modal pre-training, where image, text, and speech are aligned in a shared representation space from the start. Finally, image-text incongruity is not meme-specific: news captions, political cartoons, and short-form videos also rely on cross-modal tension. Extending MemeSonic to these domains would further test the generality of incongruity as an affective signal.
