\section{Conclusion}

We presented \textbf{MemeSonic}, a three-phase framework for meme affective understanding and
expressive audio generation. The central insight is that cross-modal incongruity—the intentional
conflict between meme image and text—is not noise to suppress but a differentiable signal to model.
We operationalize it as a continuous score $\delta$ that drives both fusion routing and TTS prosody
conditioning.

\emph{(1) Incongruity-gated fusion improves non-literal affect classification.}
FusionMoE improves Macro-F1 by up to +14.5\% over standard fusion on tasks where cross-modal
conflict is semantically load-bearing (metaphor, sarcasm). On literal tasks, simpler fusion
suffices—confirming that incongruity-aware routing is beneficial precisely where the conflict
signal carries meaning.

\emph{(2) Incongruity conditioning produces more expressive and appropriate audio.}
MemeSonic achieves AMOS 3.95 vs.\ 1.68 for Vanilla TTS (84.5\% human preference), demonstrating
that $\delta$ carries prosodic nuance beyond what discrete sentiment labels encode. The gap between
Label-Guided TTS and MemeSonic further confirms that coarse labels collapse the nuance that
incongruity preserves.

\emph{(3) Audio carries non-redundant affective signal, but alignment is critical.}
A learned linear projector lifts tri-modal sentiment accuracy from 27\% to 82\%, revealing that
the barrier to audio integration is modality-space incompatibility, not absence of affective
signal. Naive fusion yields zero improvement—the information exists in audio; it just needs to be
properly aligned.

\paragraph{Limitations.}

\emph{(1) Encoder and scale.}
Our pipeline relies on CLIP ViT-B/32, which may miss internet-specific visual semantics, and the
tri-modal study covers only 100 memes and should be read as diagnostic. Human audio evaluation is
similarly small (12 raters, 84 ratings).

\emph{(2) Evaluation subjectivity.}
Humor and sarcasm remain inherently subjective and audience-dependent. No existing dataset jointly
annotates sentiment, humor, and audio for the same memes, preventing end-to-end tri-modal
evaluation. More robust protocols should combine human preference, contextual relevance, and
safety-aware analysis rather than relying on single-axis MOS ratings.

\paragraph{Future work.}

\emph{(1) Meme-native dataset.}
The most impactful near-term contribution would be a large-scale dataset with multi-dimensional
affect annotations and paired audio. Such a resource would enable both training and evaluation of
the full MemeSonic pipeline without relying on heterogeneous benchmarks designed for adjacent tasks.

\emph{(2) Broader incongruity domains.}
The incongruity signal is not meme-specific. News captions, political cartoons, and short-form
video share the same image-text conflict structure. Extending MemeSonic to these domains—
particularly short-form video where temporal incongruity is prevalent—would demonstrate the
generality of the incongruity signal as an architectural primitive.

\emph{(3) Joint tri-modal pre-training.}
Our tri-modal alignment study reveals a fundamental obstacle: ImageBind produces near-zero cosine
similarity between its audio embeddings and TTS-synthesized speech (Top-1 accuracy $\approx 0\%$),
because its audio encoder was trained on natural soundscapes rather than expressive speech.
Bridging this gap with a post-hoc linear projector is a workaround. A more principled fix is to
train a shared encoder on meme image, text, and voiceover audio simultaneously—using
incongruity-weighted contrastive learning as a pre-training objective—so that the resulting
embeddings natively support tri-modal fusion.

\emph{(4) LLM + incongruity as structured input.}
Providing the incongruity score $\delta$ and per-modality emotion distributions as structured
features to an LLM (via function calling or tool use) could combine the commonsense reasoning
advantage of large models with the explicit conflict signal of our architecture, potentially
closing the remaining gap on tasks like intention and sarcasm where LLMs currently outperform
trained models.

\emph{(5) Retrieval, editing, and personalization.}
The same semantic--affective representation used for generation could support downstream
applications: retrieving memes by affective profile, editing prosody to shift perceived irony, or
personalizing audio output to user intent—all while preserving the original cross-modal incongruity
as a structural constraint.
