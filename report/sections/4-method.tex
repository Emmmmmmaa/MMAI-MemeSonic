\section{Proposed Method}

MemeSonic consists of three phases (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}): 
(1) Meme-to-Speech Generation, which externalizes meme affect as expressive speech; 
(2) Incongruity-Aware Fusion, which models image-text conflict for affect understanding; and 
(3) Tri-Modal Affective Fusion, which studies whether generated speech provides non-redundant affective signal.

\subsection{Phase 1: Meme-to-Speech Generation}

\paragraph{Script Generation and Prosody Prompt Adapter}
Given a meme image $v$ and text $t$, we first generate a voiceover script $s$ from the image-text context. Rather than literally describing the meme, the script verbalizes its pragmatic intent, including sarcasm, irony, humor, and affective reversal.

Discrete affect labels are too coarse to capture meme-specific delivery styles. We therefore train a Qwen3-4B-based emotion-to-prosody adapter. Given the affect label $\ell=(y^{(s)},y^{(i)})$ and image-text context $r(v,t)$, the adapter generates a prosody prompt $\pi=A_{\eta}(\ell,r(v,t))$, where $\pi$ controls tone, rhythm, emphasis, intensity, and speaking style.

\paragraph{Conditioned Speech Generation}
We synthesize meme voiceover audio by conditioning the TTS model on the script, prosody prompt, and affect label:
$a^{*}=\Psi(s,\pi,\ell)$.
This conditioning adjusts both voice timbre and intonation style according to the meme's visual-textual semantics and pragmatic affect. The resulting speech externalizes implicit meme emotion as an explicit prosodic signal, and serves as pseudo ground-truth audio for downstream understanding.

\subsection{Phase 2: Incongruity-Aware Multimodal Fusion}

\paragraph{Explicit Affect Decomposition}
We encode the meme image $v$ and text $t$ using frozen CLIP encoders $\phi_v$ and $\phi_t$. Each embedding is projected onto $C$ emotion prototypes $\{\mathbf{e}_c\}_{c=1}^C$, producing emotion distributions $p_v,p_t\in\Delta^C$:
\begin{equation}
  p^{(\cdot)}_c =
  \frac{\exp(\phi_{(\cdot)}(\cdot)^\top \mathbf{e}_c/\tau)}
       {\sum_{j=1}^C \exp(\phi_{(\cdot)}(\cdot)^\top \mathbf{e}_j/\tau)}
\end{equation}

We define image-text incongruity as:
\begin{equation}
  \delta(v,t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|p_v-p_t\|_1 \in [0,1]
\end{equation}
where larger $\delta$ indicates stronger affective conflict.

\paragraph{FusionMoE}

Different affective tasks favor different fusion assumptions. Literal sentiment often benefits from direct semantic aggregation, while sarcasm and humor require explicit modeling of image-text conflict. We therefore model meme understanding as adaptive fusion over complementary fusion strategies.

\emph{Note.} Our MoE differs from LLM-style MoE: experts correspond to explicit fusion strategies rather than learned parameter specializations. The expert set is task-dependent and can be adapted to other multimodal reasoning tasks.

\textbf{Expert R (Concat Fusion).}
This expert performs standard representation-level fusion through direct feature concatenation:
\begin{equation}
  z_R=[\phi_v(v);\phi_t(t)]
\end{equation}
It captures aligned semantic signals and serves as a strong baseline for literal or weakly incongruent memes.

\textbf{Expert U (Late Fusion).}
This expert performs decision-level fusion by averaging unimodal predictions:
\begin{equation}
  z_U=\tfrac{1}{2}(W_v\phi_v(v)+W_t\phi_t(t))
\end{equation}
It is more robust when one modality dominates or when image-text interaction is weak.

\textbf{Expert S (Incongruity Fusion).}
This expert explicitly models cross-modal conflict using emotion disagreement and incongruity features:
\begin{equation}
  z_S=\text{MLP}([\phi_v(v);\phi_t(t);p_v-p_t;\delta])
\end{equation}
It is designed for sarcasm, irony, and other non-literal memes where affect emerges from image-text tension.

A router predicts soft expert weights:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{r}=
  \text{softmax}(W_r[\phi_v(v);\phi_t(t);\delta]+b_r)
\end{equation}

The final representation is computed as:
\begin{equation}
  z=r_R z_R+r_U z_U+r_S z_S
\end{equation}


\subsection{Phase 3: Tri-Modal Affective Fusion}

We next incorporate generated speech as a third affective modality for meme understanding. Naive tri-modal fusion fails because speech and vision-language embeddings lie in incompatible representation spaces. In particular, ImageBind targets general audio semantics rather than expressive speech.

To bridge this gap, we train a linear projector $W\in\mathbb{R}^{D_a\times D_c}$ that maps Emotion2Vec speech embeddings into the shared CLIP/ImageBind semantic space:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{a}=\text{normalize}(W\phi_a(a^*)+b)
\end{equation}

The projector is supervised using MSE loss against the sentiment prototype of the sample:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{proj}}
  =
  \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N
  \|\hat{a}_i-\text{normalize}(\mathbf{e}_{y_i^{(s)}})\|_2^2
\end{equation}

After projection, image, text, and speech embeddings are fused in the shared semantic space for cosine-similarity classification. This experiment isolates modality mismatch from signal quality, showing that generated speech becomes a useful affective modality once properly aligned.
