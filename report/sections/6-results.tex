\subsubsection{Key Findings}

\textbf{Incongruity Fusion excels on conflict-heavy tasks.}
On MET-Meme/metaphor, Incongruity Fusion achieves 87.6\% accuracy and 87.6\% Macro-F1—a
+5.8\% accuracy gain over the best standard fusion (Concat) and +12.0\% over Unimodal-Text.
This is the most semantically incongruent task in our benchmark, validating our core hypothesis
that explicit conflict modeling is necessary for non-literal affect.
On MMSD/sarcasm, Incongruity Fusion achieves 84.0\% F1, matching MMOE and outperforming
Concat by +1.4\%.

\textbf{MMOE achieves the best balance across diverse tasks.}
LLM-style MMOE with 4 experts achieves the best or tied-best trained model on 6/10 tasks
(MET/hate, MET/metaphor, Memotion/offensive, Memotion/sentiment, MMSD/sarcasm). Its advantage
is most pronounced on tasks with mixed interaction types—where routing adaptively selects between
concat and incongruity experts per sample.

\textbf{Standard fusion can regress on literal tasks.}
On Memotion/humour, Concat and Late Fusion slightly outperform Incongruity Fusion. This is
consistent with our theory: humour in Memotion is coded at a binary level (funny/not), where
the cross-modal conflict \emph{is} the humour, but the classifier benefits more from pooling
shared semantic content than from explicitly modeling the conflict vector.

\textbf{LLMs excel at open-ended tasks but fail on structured conflict.}
LLMs achieve the best overall accuracy on MET/intention (o4-mini: 56\%) and Memotion/sarcasm
(Qwen3: 49\% F1), tasks that benefit from commonsense reasoning. However, trained models
outperform all LLMs on MET/metaphor (+14.5\% F1), Memotion/motivational (+14.2\% acc), and
Memotion/sentiment (+27.1\% acc), where nuanced distribution modeling matters more than reasoning.

\textbf{Key findings.}

(EMOS) improves from 2.35 to 3.82—a gain of +1.47 points—showing that the adapter successfully

while MemeSonic uses the full incongruity-conditioned representation. This demonstrates that
\emph{the cross-modal conflict signal carries information not captured by the sentiment label alone}.

\subsubsection{RQ2: Does Audio Improve Classification? The Modality Gap Problem}

Table~\ref{tab:rq2} and Figure~\ref{fig:rq2} show the impact of audio integration.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Sentiment and intention accuracy under different fusion configurations. Naive tri-modal fusion
yields zero improvement over V+T; the linear projector unlocks audio's contribution.}
\label{tab:rq2}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
\textbf{Configuration} & \textbf{Sentiment Acc} & \textbf{Intention Acc} \\
\midrule
V only                        & 0.15 & 0.38 \\
V + T (baseline)              & 0.27 & 0.45 \\
V + T + A (naive, all 9 settings) & 0.27 & 0.45 \\
\midrule
V + T + A\textsubscript{proj} (mean audio) & \textbf{0.82} & \textbf{0.49} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Naive fusion completely fails.} All 9 voice-setting variants of V+T+A produce identical
accuracy to V+T (sentiment: 0.27, intention: 0.45), with 0.0\% prediction flip rate across
voice settings. The root cause is a modality-space incompatibility: Emotion2Vec and ImageBind
are trained on fundamentally different data distributions, and zero-padding ImageBind prototypes
with zeros for the audio dimensions renders audio invisible to cosine similarity classification.

\textbf{The linear projector bridges the modality gap.} After projecting Emotion2Vec embeddings
into ImageBind space, sentiment accuracy jumps from 0.27 to 0.82 (+0.55), and the prediction
flip rate rises to 46.0\% for sentiment and 14.0\% for intention—confirming that audio now
actively participates in classification.

\textbf{Caveat: the +0.55 gain reflects alignment, not generalization.} The projector learns to
map any audio of a given GT sentiment toward that class's ImageBind prototype, effectively using
class-supervision to encode the label into the audio embedding. With only 900 training samples
(720 train), this is partly a memorization of label structure rather than a generalizable
cross-modal alignment. The key scientific contribution is the diagnostic: \emph{the information
exists in audio; the modality gap—not a lack of signal—is the barrier to fusion}.

\subsubsection{Intra-Modality Distance Analysis}

Intra-sentiment cosine distance in Emotion2Vec space (0.03--0.19) is substantially lower than
intra-voice-setting distance (0.15--0.17), confirming that Emotion2Vec encodes semantic emotion
content rather than prosodic style variation. This explains why voice-setting changes do not affect
predictions (prosody is absorbed by Emotion2Vec into emotion-semantic clusters) and why the
per-class audio agreement is driven by script content (which carries GT sentiment) rather than
by the voice rendering.

\subsection{Discussion and Error Analysis}

\textbf{Where Incongruity Fusion fails.}
Incongruity Fusion underperforms on MET/intention ($-15.6\%$ F1 vs.\ Unimodal-Image) and
Memotion/humour ($-8.9\%$ F1 vs.\ Late Fusion). In both cases, the incongruity signal introduces
additional noise when the task requires holistic understanding rather than conflict exploitation.
Intention classification (interactive, entertaining, etc.) is often determined by the distributional
properties of the image-text pair, not by their emotional contrast. Future work could gate the
incongruity feature adaptively per task.

\textbf{LLMs and reasoning models.}
The strong performance of o4-mini (CoT) on Memotion/offensive (59.9\% F1) and sarcasm (44.0\% F1)
suggests that chain-of-thought reasoning provides substantial benefit for tasks requiring social
context—an advantage unavailable to our CLIP-based trained models. Combining LLM reasoning with
our incongruity signal (e.g., as a structured prompt feature) is a promising direction.

\textbf{Emotion2Vec vs. ImageBind for audio.}
ImageBind is trained on environmental sounds and achieves effectively zero performance on TTS
speech alignment (V$\leftrightarrow$T Top-1 = 0.98; A$\leftrightarrow$T Top-1 $\approx$ 0.00
in our probing). Emotion2Vec is trained on emotional speech and captures sentiment-discriminative
structure but not prosodic style variation. Neither model is ideal for meme voiceover; a model
trained on emotionally annotated TTS speech would be needed for complete tri-modal integration.
